# Conduct: what Amazon penalises, third-party filers and agents

## The conduct paragraph

- Amazon prohibits activity that interferes with its capacity to help other sellers; its examples are "submitting insufficiently researched or premature requests" and "submitting a large number of requests in a short time" (FL11).
- Sellers who repeat them "may receive delayed support on their cases or be subject to monitoring, investigation, and account action" (FL11).
- The paragraph stands in the 2021-04 UK-path policy PDF ("high volumes") and the 2025-02-24 US PDF, and Amazon's own messages quoted it in the Canada store in 2022 and in the US in 2024 (FL11).
- Amazon's 2022 Canada denial already used the later wording and added a catch-all, "or otherwise engaging in any other activity that interferes with our capacity to help other sellers" (FL42).
- The Supply Chain Portal policy carries its own version for MCF accounts, and for inbound adds that merchants generating many shipping problems may face investigation, monitoring and delayed reimbursements; no FBA page read carries that sentence (FL11, FL41).
- Selling Partner Support's restatement: "we decline requests that are not consistent with our policies", and "Amazon may close cases or limit support if you don't follow these guidelines" (FL12).
- The Seller Code of Conduct requires sellers to "Provide accurate information to Amazon … at all times" and to "Act fairly and not misuse Amazon's features or services", words that reach this pack through an Amazon staff member's quotation of an unread page (FL15).

## What sellers report

- Deactivation notices sellers have posted for claims name Section 3 of the BSA, the Seller Code of Conduct, "FBA credit reimbursement abuse" and "repeatedly submitted improper claims for FBA reimbursement" (FL16).
- Sellers deactivated for claims report having used a third-party claims service, and one found the service still filing while the account was deactivated (FL17).
- DAM Law Firm writes that from 2026-03-04 the seller answers for every agent on the account; no Amazon page read states that the seller answers for a service's claims (FL17).
- Practitioners report a deactivation after an inbound shortfall was paid and the "found" units proved not to be the seller's (FL18).

## Third parties and apps

- No Amazon page read bars or endorses third-party reimbursement services (FL19).
- SP-API AUP 2.11 binds an app acting for a seller to keep all submissions and communications "accurate, policy-compliant" and free of "frivolous or non-compliant contacts or degradation of Amazon support systems"; AUP 1.1 limits it to activities the seller has authorised (FL20).
- An SP-API developer that needs Amazon Portal access asks the seller to grant it through secondary user permissions, and must not request or share Amazon Portal usernames or passwords (FL21).

## The agent duties from 2026-03-04

- From 2026-03-04 the BSA requires any automated software or AI agent accessing Amazon Services to identify itself as automated, comply with the Agent Policy and cease access when Amazon asks; Amazon may restrict its access, and continued use is acceptance (FL22).
- Amazon announced it in a US thread on 2026-02-17 and a Canada thread on 2026-02-19 (FL22).
- The Agent Policy's own text is unread; the user-agent string, the bar on mimicking human keystrokes and truthful answers are a practitioner's relay, and what the policy says beyond the three duties is read in Amazon's documents, not yet seen on a live account (FL22).
- Amazon's own `support-case-helper` skill fills a "Reported From" field with the running agent's name (FL23).
